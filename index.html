1. Overview & Square Structure KLXHY pg 8

  • Chinese characters (Hànzì) are the written symbols of the Chinese language.
  • They are composed of basic strokes and written in a specific order.
  • Their structure evolved from ancient pictographs or symbols (e.g. ancient water symbol → shuǐ).
  • All characters fit into equal-sized square grids regardless of stroke count, maintaining a well-balanced structure.

3. Strokes with Turns (zhé) KLXHY pg 8, 10

A turn (zhé) is a continuous change of direction without lifting your pen.

Stroke Name Rice Grid Symbol Key Technique & Visual Hack Example
héng piě
Horizontal start, then curve smoothly into a left-falling curve. 又
piě diǎn
Slant down-left, then turn with a wide open angle to bottom-right. 女
shù tí
Straight vertical down, then a short lift up towards top right. 以
héng zhé
Write like the number "7" with a sharp 90-degree corner. 亡
shù zhé
Write like the capital letter "L" with a sharp 90-degree corner. 云
piě zhé
Write like a slanted "V" (V senget) turning horizontal right. 车

4. Strokes with Hooks (gōu) KLXHY pg 8, 10

A hook (gōu) is a short, sharp flick of the pen at the end of a stroke.

Stroke Name Rice Grid Symbol Key Technique & Visual Hack Example
héng gōu
Horizontal line with a short, sharp flick down-left. 买
shù gōu
Keep vertical line straight. Do NOT curve like English "j/g"! 小
xié gōu
"xié" = senget (slanted). Slant down-right, hook points to 12 o'clock! 戈
wò gōu
"wò" = lie down. Draw a lay-down bracket, hook points inward. 心
wān gōu
"wān" = curved. Write like a close/right bracket ")" before hooking up. 了
shù wān gōu
Down, smooth curve right, and flick up. Make the bottom curve smooth! 七

7. General Rules for Writing Characters KLXHY pg 9

Characters must be written according to fixed stroke order rules:

Rules for Crossing Strokes

1. héng before shù

Horizontal before vertical.

十 (shí)、木 (mù)
2. piě before nà

Left-falling before right-falling.

人 (rén)、大 (dà)

Rules for Writing Order

3. From top to bottom
三 (sān)、劳 (láo)
4. From left to right
川 (chuān)、什 (shén)
5. From outside to inside
月 (yuè)、问 (wèn)

Rules for Frames & Symmetry

6. Fill inside before sealing frame

Put items inside before closing door!

四 (sì)、国 (guó)
7. Middle precedes two sides

Symmetrical characters center first.

小 (xiǎo)、水 (shuǐ)
8. Skewering vertical stroke last

Vertical passing middle comes last.

手 (shǒu)、事 (shì)

8. Character Structure Types KLXHY pg 9

Characters are divided into Single-Component (1 part, e.g. 马, 月, 木, 人) and Multi-Component (2+ parts).

Structure Name Visual Concept Layout Examples
Left-Right Structure 好 (hǎo)、你 (nǐ)、谢 (xiè)
Top-Bottom Structure 字 (zì)、早 (zǎo)、点 (diǎn)
Triangle Structure 森 (sēn)、品 (pǐn)
Enclosed Structure (Semi / Fully) Semi: 店、句、进、网、区
Fully: 国、四、凶

Ex E: True (√) or False (x) Statements (Page 13) KLXHY pg 13

1. "Gōu (hook)" is part of its stroke, never a separate stroke.
True (√) — Hooks are attached to a stroke, not written separately.
2. "Héng zhé" is treated as one continuous stroke.
True (√) — Turns are written without lifting pen.
3. Left-falling strokes are written after right-falling strokes.
False (x) — Rule: piě (left-falling) comes BEFORE nà (right-falling).
4. Horizontal strokes are usually written before vertical strokes.
True (√) — Rule: héng before shù.
5. A single-component character contains only one component.
True (√) — It cannot be split into separate parts.
6. A character like "明 (míng)" has a semi-enclosed structure.
False (x) — "明" is a Left-Right structure (日 + 月).
7. In symmetrical characters, the middle stroke is written before the two sides.
True (√) — Rule: Middle precedes the two sides (e.g. 小, 水).
8. In enclosed characters, the closing frame is written before the inner strokes.
False (x) — Rule: Fill inside BEFORE sealing the frame.

10. Lesson Wrap-Up & Key Takeaways

Core Points Summary:

  • Continuous Writing: All strokes with turns (zhé) and hooks (gōu) must be written in one continuous movement without lifting your pen.
  • Square Balance: Fill the imaginary square grid evenly, ensuring proper proportions whether the character has 2 or 10 strokes.
  • Stroke Order Master Rules: Remember héng before shù, piě before nà, and fill inside before sealing enclosed frames!
