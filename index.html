1. Overview & Square Structure KLXHY pg 8

  • Chinese characters (Hànzì) are the written symbols of the Chinese language.[cite: 1]
  • They are composed of basic strokes and written in a specific order.[cite: 1]
  • Their structure evolved from ancient pictographs or symbols (e.g. ancient water symbol → shuǐ).[cite: 1]
  • All characters fit into equal-sized square grids regardless of stroke count, maintaining a well-balanced structure.[cite: 1]
Key Concept — The Equal Room Rule:
Think of the square Rice Grid as a room with a fixed size[cite: 1]. Whether a character has only 1 stroke (e.g. 一) or 15 strokes (e.g. 餐), it must be centered and balanced to fit comfortably in the room![cite: 1]

Core Concept: "What You Read Is What You Write!"

The Chinese Stroke Naming Secret

Never feel overwhelmed by long stroke names like héng zhé gōu or shù tí!
Chinese stroke names are NOT random terms — they are literally step-by-step writing instructions!

How to Break Down ANY Complex Stroke Name:

Every complex stroke is built by combining basic stroke names with 2 action modifiers:

Action Modifier #1: zhé

zhé means "TURN" or "CHANGE DIRECTION"![cite: 2]
Whenever you see "zhé", bend your pen direction without lifting it.[cite: 2]

Action Modifier #2: gōu

gōu means "HOOK"![cite: 2]
Whenever a stroke name ends with "gōu", flick a short sharp hook at the end.[cite: 2]

Formula Breakdown Examples:

Stroke Name Formula Breakdown What You Do
héng zhé héng + zhé Draw horizontal line → TURN DOWN!
shù tí shù + tí Draw vertical line → LIFT UP-RIGHT!
héng gōu héng + gōu Draw horizontal line → FLICK A HOOK!
shù zhé zhé gōu shù + zhé + zhé + gōu Vertical → Turn → Turn → Hook! (e.g. in 马)
Pro Tip for Practice:
Before writing any character, say the stroke names aloud and chant the actions together: "Héng... TURN! Shù... HOOK!" Reading the names aloud will directly guide your pen!

7. General Rules for Writing Characters KLXHY pg 9

Characters must be written according to fixed stroke order rules:[cite: 1]

Memory Hack — Building a House Analogy:
Think of stroke order like constructing a house:
1. Build the roof first (→ Top to bottom)
2. Erect the main walls (→ Left to right / Horizontal before vertical)
3. Move furniture inside the room (→ Inside component first)
4. Finally, lock the front door! (→ Seal enclosed frame last)

8. Character Structure Types KLXHY pg 9

Characters are divided into Single-Component (1 part, e.g. 马, 月, 木, 人) and Multi-Component (2+ parts).[cite: 1]

Structural Concept — Lego Bricks Analogy:
Multi-component characters are assembled like Lego blocks! Instead of memorizing 20 separate strokes, you only need to recognize 2 or 3 modular components fitted into a specific layout (Left-Right, Top-Bottom, Enclosed, Triangle).

10. Lesson Wrap-Up & Key Takeaways

Core Points Summary:

  • "What You Read Is What You Write": Decode complex stroke names by treating basic stroke names as action steps (zhé = turn, gōu = hook).
  • Continuous Writing: All strokes with turns (zhé) and hooks (gōu) must be written in one continuous movement without lifting your pen.[cite: 2]
  • Square Balance: Fill the imaginary square grid evenly, ensuring proper proportions whether the character has 2 or 10 strokes.[cite: 1]
  • Stroke Order Master Rules: Remember héng before shù, piě before nà, and fill inside before sealing enclosed frames![cite: 1]
