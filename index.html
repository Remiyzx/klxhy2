1. What are Chinese Characters? KLXHY pg 8

Chinese characters (Hanzi) are the written symbols of the Chinese language. Unlike English letters, they are essentially pictures and symbols that evolved over thousands of years!

  • They are made up of basic strokes written in a specific order.
  • Their structure evolved from ancient pictographs or symbols.
    Evolution of Water: Ancient symbol → shuǐ (water)
  • All characters fit perfectly into equal-sized square grids. No matter if a character has 2 strokes or 20 strokes, its structure must be well balanced.

2. The 6 Basic Strokes KLXHY pg 8, 10

A stroke is a single continuous movement of a pen. These are the foundations of all Chinese characters.

Teacher's Rule #1:
Write each stroke in ONE continuous movement! Do not lift your pen until the stroke is finished.

4. Strokes with Hooks (gōu) KLXHY pg 8, 10

Another type of stroke ends with a hook (gōu). It is basically a little flick of the pen at the very end of the stroke.

Remi's Slide Hacks for Hooks:
Do the hook short and sharp!
For wò gōu (wò = lie down), don't start with a vertical line. Just draw a "lay-down bracket".

Spot the Differences: Confusing Strokes!

Practice carefully! Compare similar strokes and focus on the small differences (hooks, curves, lifts).

  • shù gōu (straight) vs. xié gōu (slanted) vs. wān gōu (curved)
  • piě zhé (slanted V) vs. piě diǎn (wide open angle)
  • shù gōu (hooks left) vs. shù tí (lifts right)
  • héng zhé tí (lifts right/up) vs. héng zhé gōu (hooks left/in)
  • héng xié gōu (straight slant) vs. héng zhé wān gōu (rounded curve)

7. General Rules for Writing Characters KLXHY pg 9

As squared symbols, Chinese characters must be written according to fixed rules known as stroke order. Just like building a house, you need a foundation first!

Rules for Crossing Strokes

1. héng before shù

Horizontal before vertical.

十 (shí)
2. piě before nà

Left-falling before right-falling.

人 (rén)
Note: This rule is ONLY for crossing strokes. If the strokes do not cross, follow the character's specific stroke order!

Rules for Direction

3. Top to Bottom
三 (sān)
4. Left to Right
什 (shén)
5. Outside to Inside
月 (yuè)

Rules for Frames & Symmetrical Shapes

6. Fill inside before sealing the frame

Put people in the room before closing the door!

国 (guó)
7. Middle precedes the two sides

Applies to symmetrical characters.

小 (xiǎo)
8. Skewering stroke last

Vertical hook stroke passing through the middle comes last.

中 (zhōng)

8. Character Structures KLXHY pg 9

In terms of structure, characters can be divided into two main types: Single-Component and Multi-Component.

1. Single-Component Characters:
Made of only ONE part. They cannot be split.
Examples: mǎ (马), yuè (月), mù (木), rén (人)
2. Multi-Component Characters:
Made of TWO OR MORE parts built together like Lego bricks.

Exercise E: True or False KLXHY pg 13

1. "Gōu (hook)" is part of its stroke, never a separate stroke.
True (✔)
2. "Héng zhé" is treated as one continuous stroke.
True (✔)
3. Left-falling strokes are written after right-falling strokes.
False (✖). Rule: piě (left-falling) before nà (right-falling).
4. Horizontal strokes are usually written before vertical strokes.
True (✔). Rule: héng before shù.
5. A character like "míng" has a semi-enclosed structure.
False (✖). "míng" is a Left-Right structure.
6. In enclosed characters, the closing frame is written before the inner strokes.
False (✖). Rule: Fill the inside BEFORE sealing the frame.
